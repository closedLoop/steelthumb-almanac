# Care for and investigate sunflowers

Applies to [sunflower](../crops/sunflower.md). Annual Helianthus annuus for seed or flowers; ground culture and compact selections in outdoor bags.

[Outdoor grow bags](../systems/outdoor-grow-bags.md) describes the shared setup and adaptation limits.

## Before you start

Retain cultivar height, flower/seed goal, and planting record; inspect the support and moisture condition.

## Steps and source settings

1. Apply dry-weather care and maintain access around the plants.
2. Check stems and supports after wind, especially for bags with flexible rims.
3. If leaves are sticky or chewed, inspect for insects before selecting an intervention. If leaves are spotted, record position and progression rather than naming a disease from a photograph.
4. For empty or missing seed, distinguish poor filling from observed feeding; the collection does not diagnose either from seed count alone.

<a id="dry-weather"></a>
Illinois recommends watering in dry weather and controlling weeds while sunflowers are young.[^illinois-sunflower]

<a id="wind-support"></a>
Shelter or stake tall, heavy-headed sunflowers against wind damage.[^illinois-sunflower]

<a id="pest-signs"></a>
Aphids can leave honeydew; caterpillars can chew sunflower leaves and petals.[^umn-sunflower]

## Investigate: sticky or chewed foliage

These are editorial observation steps using the linked source claims.

1. Inspect affected surfaces for the organisms associated with the [candidate signs](#pest-signs). Record insects seen separately from damage.
2. Seek identification for unresolved insects before treatment. On the next inspection, compare the same leaves and new growth; persistent chewing without a visible organism remains unresolved.

## Follow-up

Record stem damage, new leaves, flower stage, and evidence of feeding; revisit affected plants after any change. Use [harvest](harvest-sunflower.md) for readiness.

## Limits

No insecticide recommendation, bag fertilizer rate, or pollination guarantee is included. Plant size and ornamental flowering alone do not establish edible yield.
