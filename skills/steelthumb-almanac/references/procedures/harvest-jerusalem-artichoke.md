# Harvest and store Jerusalem artichokes

Applies to [Jerusalem artichoke](../crops/jerusalem-artichoke.md), in soil or [grow bags](../systems/jerusalem-artichoke-grow-bags.md). Prepare labels, harvest containers, a garden fork for ground crops, a contained sorting surface for bags, and storage space. A scale is optional.

## Lift and sort

<a id="harvest-season"></a>
1. USask favors late fall after a killing frost and recommends loosening soil with a fork before lifting.[^usask] Use local plant stage and weather; this is not a universal day count.
2. For bags, the Almanac adaptation is to open or empty the bag onto a contained sorting surface, recover tubers, and inspect the emptied bag and underlying area. This handling method has not been field-tested here. Do not assume any remaining medium is tuber-free.
3. Separate food, planting stock, damaged material, and unknown-condition material. Record their masses separately if measuring yield; do not equate whole-plant weight with edible harvest.

<a id="refrigerated-storage"></a>
4. Johnny’s recommends refrigerating in perforated bags, maintaining dampness without wetness.[^johnny] No storage-duration promise or freezing/canning process is provided here.

<a id="replant-tubers"></a>
5. Retain labelled tubers for the [next planting](plant-jerusalem-artichoke.md).[^johnny] Winter handling for an exposed bag is unresolved; do not assume its contents receive the protection of garden soil.

## Follow-up and use

As Almanac observation prompts, inspect stored tubers for deterioration and record losses and elapsed storage time. Keep uncertain or decayed material out of any claimed edible-yield total. Record bag dimensions, starting tuber count, harvest date, and cultivar with harvest results.

Johnny’s describes raw and cooked food use.[^johnny] Nutrient values, health effects, digestive-tolerance treatments, and tested long-term preservation need separate evidence and are not established by that description.

Next spring, check the former planting location for volunteers. A harvest record documents one result, not the effectiveness of all steps. No actual yield or successful overwintering is claimed in this procedure.
