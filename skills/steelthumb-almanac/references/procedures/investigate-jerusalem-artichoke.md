# Investigate Jerusalem artichoke problems

Applies to [Jerusalem artichoke](../crops/jerusalem-artichoke.md) and [grow bags](../systems/jerusalem-artichoke-grow-bags.md). Use a planting record, recent care history, and photographs if available. The checks below are editorial diagnostic prompts; they have not been validated as a diagnostic key.

## Observe before changing care

1. Record which plants or tubers are affected, timing, growth stage, recent weather, medium moisture, and recent changes. Inspect more than one place in a bag before describing the whole medium as dry or wet.
2. Choose the closest concern below. Record observations separately from suspected causes.

| Observation | Candidate or evidence limit | Useful next observation or action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wilt or yellowing | The container reference warns against poor drainage and notes that dark containers heat more; neither establishes the cause of this plant's symptoms.[^illinois] | Inspect moisture, drainage, and exposure; use [care](care-for-jerusalem-artichoke.md) for the next action. |
| Leaning or a moving bag | Support adequacy is unresolved for the actual setup. | Check stems, anchoring, and bag position against the [system precautions](../systems/jerusalem-artichoke-grow-bags.md). |
| Few tubers | No bag-yield baseline has been established. | Retain dates, cultivar, bag dimensions, tuber count, water history, and [harvest basis](harvest-jerusalem-artichoke.md); do not declare fertilizer deficiency from yield alone. |
| Eaten tubers | Rodent feeding is one documented possibility.[^johnny] | Look for burrows and direct evidence; do not identify a pest from missing tissue alone. |
| New shoots outside the intended planting | The crop's spreading habit is documented.[^mu] | Confirm plant identity and inspect the planting boundary and displaced medium. |
| Soft, discolored, or otherwise abnormal tissue | A pathogen has not been established by this collection. | Record tissue location and progression; seek local diagnostic help before choosing a disease treatment. |

<a id="rodent-candidate"></a>
Rodents are a candidate for tuber losses, particularly in winter.[^johnny] That possibility is not a diagnosis.

## Follow-up

3. Choose one observation or care correction where practical. Record the intended response and when the next check will occur; no universal recovery interval is asserted.
4. Compare new observations with the baseline, including deterioration or no change. Use the [garden-observation workflow](review-garden-observations.md) to preserve uncertainty.

Pest-specific control, laboratory disease identification, and bag-yield thresholds remain gaps. This entry point supplies no pesticide treatment or invented diagnosis.
